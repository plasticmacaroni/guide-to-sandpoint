1 The Setup at a Glance

WhereSandpoint, a coastal town on Varisia's Lost Coast, on the western shore of the Varisian Gulf, ~50 miles north of the city of Magnimar.
WhenThe year 4724 AR — 17 years after the events of "Burnt Offerings," the original Rise of the Runelords opener.
Who you areEstablished 4th-level local adventurers with a minor regional reputation.
The hookThe town's most powerful cleric, Abstalar Zantus, gathers your group to investigate an unsettling mystery in the town graveyard (the Boneyard).
The stakesSandpoint has survived two decades of disasters; people whisper the town is cursed. The twist: there isn't one doom looming over Sandpoint — there are seven.
ToneSandpoint is a neutral-good, friendly, welcoming town; the threats are decidedly evil. Expect underground dungeon-crawling punctuated by social & investigation play back in town.

2 History & Timeline

One of Golarion's oldest established adventuring towns — a past layered with prosperity and tragedy.

Before Recorded History — The Age of Thassilon
  • The Azlanti exile Emperor Xin founds the empire of Thassilon on rune magic, appointing seven governors who become the immortal runelords — each bound to one of seven sins and one school of magic, all marked by the Sihedron star.
  • The runelords murder Xin and rule as tyrants for millennia, until the apocalypse of Earthfall shatters Thassilon. Several runelords escape into magical stasis to wait out the ages — the seed of every doom that follows.
AR 4666 — Sandpoint Is Founded
  • Four noble families — the Deverins, Kaijitsus, Scarnettis, and Valdemars — pool resources to settle the Lost Coast, forming the Sandpoint Mercantile League and dividing the town's commerce among themselves.
  • A natural harbor, fertile land, and nearness to Magnimar make it thrive — but the chosen cove is already claimed by a local Varisian tribe.
AR 4666 — The Varisian Conflict & the Compact
  • Negotiations stall, then Alamon Scarnetti — acting without sanction — leads a drunken raid on the Varisian camp, leaving dead on both sides. Magnimar's Varisian Council intervenes.
  • Bard Almah Deverin brokers a compromise: the Varisians waive their land claim for 40 years of profit-sharing (plus worship of Desna in the town cathedral). That compact expires AR 4726 — two years from now, and the Scarnettis are pushing to end it.
AR 4702 — The Late Unpleasantness: The Chopper
  • Over a single winter a serial killer — "The Chopper" — leaves a body every few days, each missing hands, feet, eyes, and tongue. 25 victims in all.
  • Sheriff Casp Avertin mortally wounds him in Chopper's Alley at the cost of his own life; deputy Belor Hemlock finishes the hunt and is named Sheriff.
AR 4702 — The Sandpoint Fire
  • Months later the Sandpoint Chapel burns down; beloved priest Father Tobyn dies, and his adopted daughter Nualia is presumed dead.
  • Two catastrophes in one year give the era its name — "The Late Unpleasantness." The chapel is later rebuilt as the grander Sandpoint Cathedral.
AR 4707 — The Year of Heroes
  • Three crises strike in a single year, each turned back by a band of visiting adventurers who become local legends (the famous earlier Adventure Paths):
  • The Goblin Raid: the Thistletop tribe attacks during the Swallowtail Festival consecrating the new Cathedral.
  • The Skinsaw Murders: ritual killings marked with Sihedron stars, traced to a conspiracy in Magnimar.
  • The Giant Raid: Stone Giants led by a red dragon seize citizens in what feels like a deliberate strike. Behind it all: Runelord Karzoug and the return of Thassilon.
AR 4712 — The Licktoad Affair & Ameiko's Departure
  • The Licktoad goblins of Brinestump Marsh raid with a stolen cache of fireworks; Ameiko Kaijitsu and companions end the threat.
  • Soon after, Ameiko sails for Tian Xia — eventually becoming Empress of Minkai. She has not returned.
AR 4713 — The Tsunami & The Pit
  • A great wave devastates the Lost Coast; Sandpoint rebuilds, as ever.
  • At the same moment, a massive sinkhole tears open the plateau east of town, exposing a vast subterranean ruin locals call The Pit — now monster-claimed and strongly off-limits.
Backdrop — Karzoug & the Return of Thassilon
  • Runelord Karzoug's awakening (amid the 4707 crises) revealed that ancient Thassilon had returned; adventurers flooded Varisia to plunder its ruins. He was destroyed at Xin-Shalast before he could fully cross back.
AR 4717–4718 — The Return of the Runelords
  • Runelord Alaznist of Wrath wakes beneath Hollow Mountain, seizes the time-bending Scepter of Ages, and travels into ancient Thassilon to rewrite history in her favor — murdering the slumbering Xanderghul (the Peacock Spirit) to remove her oldest rival.
  • A second band of heroes chases her edits through time, mends the broken timeline from a city outside of time, and destroys her — aided by the awakened, redeemed Sorshen.
AR 4719 — New Thassilon Rises
  • A genuinely new nation appears in the Varisian wilds — New Thassilon — co-ruled by two awakened runelords: Sorshen over Eurythnia and Belimarius over Edasseril. It did not "always exist"; it arrived.
  • Of the seven, two — Krune (Sloth) and Zutha (Gluttony) — have never woken. As of the latest word, they slumber still.
AR 4724 — Now
  • After two decades of murders, fires, raids, giants, tsunamis, and worse, many quietly believe the town is cursed.
  • The compact expires in two years, old tensions simmer — and Sandpoint faces not one doom, but seven.

3 Ancient Thassilon & the Runelords

The deep backdrop. Understanding this makes the whole campaign click.

The Empire

Thassilon

An ancient magical empire that ruled this land before recorded history — before the cataclysm of Earthfall — governed by arcane tyrants called runelords (e.g. Karzoug, Alaznist).

Its Dark Craft

Fleshwarping

Thassilon's runelords warped flesh to create monstrous servants and guardians. Its most widespread modern legacy is the sinspawn; rarer fleshwarps survive on the Lost Coast too.

Where It Survives

Ruins of the Empire

The Old Light (a collapsed Thassilonian tower in Sandpoint, NW near Junk Beach), Magnimar's monoliths, Riddleport's rune-arch, Hollow Mountain, the Lady's Light, and Guiltspur.

Why It Matters

The Thassilonian Tongue

The Thassilonian language is genuinely useful here. Magic, ancient ruins, and runelord lore recur throughout — and inspired the town's new Runewatchers faction.

Emperor Xin & the Seven Virtues

Thassilon was founded by Emperor Xin, an exile from older Azlant who built his empire on rune magic — what his priests of Lissala called the language of creation. Xin organized all magic around seven virtues of rule and appointed seven gifted wizards to govern in his name. Over the centuries those virtues curdled into sins — wealth into greed, love into lust, righteous anger into wrath — and his governors became the immortal tyrants known as runelords. When Xin reached for immortality, the seven sent the rune giant Shasthaak to murder him; the botched ritual annihilated emperor, assassin, and palace alike, and the runelords carved the empire into seven realms of their own.

The Sihedron — Seven Sins, Seven Schools

The seven-pointed Sihedron star binds it all together: seven sins, each tied to one school of magic and one runelord. A runelord grew vastly powerful within their own sin-school but deliberately crippled in its opposites — wrath's evocation could not abide abjuration or conjuration, and so on. The star carved on victims, ruins, and relics alike is the signature of this whole system.

How the Runelords Came Back — and the Time That Broke

Greed first. Karzoug was the first to stir: from beyond the world he reached through the Eye of Avarice above his frozen capital Xin-Shalast, while a Sihedron cult — the lamia Xanesha and the stone-giant warlord Mokmurian at Jorgenfist — harvested souls and raised armies to fund his return. The heroes of those years (the Rise of the Runelords) armed themselves in the lost laboratory of Runeforge, climbed to Xin-Shalast, and destroyed him before he could fully cross back.

Then wrath. A decade later Alaznist woke beneath Hollow Mountain and seized the Scepter of Ages, an artifact of time. She travelled into ancient Thassilon itself, rewriting history to her advantage — and reached the refuge where Xanderghul, the Peacock Spirit, still slept, murdering her oldest rival in his sleep. Because she edited the past, even the memory of "how things were" changed with it. A second band of heroes (the Return of the Runelords) chased her alterations through time, repaired each one from a city outside of time, and brought her down at Hollow Mountain — with help from an unexpected quarter.

New Thassilon is new. When the dust settled, a runelord realm rose again in the Varisian wilds — but treat it as a genuinely new arrival, not a quiet retcon. History wasn't smoothed over so everyone "always remembered" it standing there; New Thassilon simply appeared, co-ruled by two awakened (and partly reformed) runelords: Sorshen over Eurythnia and Belimarius over Edasseril.

Two still sleep. Of the seven, Krune (Sloth) and Zutha (Gluttony) have never woken in the modern age. As of the latest word reaching Sandpoint, they slumber still.

Where the Seven Stand Now

Greed · destroyed

Karzoug

Raised ~4707. First to reach back into the world; destroyed by the Rise-of-the-Runelords party at Xin-Shalast before he could fully return. A historic rival of Alaznist (their realms of greed and wrath bordered each other), and the betrayer who left Belimarius sealed outside time.

Wrath · destroyed

Alaznist

Raised ~4717. Woke beneath Hollow Mountain; used the Scepter of Ages to rewrite history and to murder the sleeping Xanderghul. Sought to crown herself over a remade empire — until the Return-of-the-Runelords party mended time and destroyed her.

Pride · slain by Alaznist

Xanderghul

Raised, then killed ~4718. Mightiest and longest-reigning of the seven; secretly the cult-god Peacock Spirit, and (with Sorshen) an architect of Xin's murder long ago. The heroes confronted his temple — but Alaznist reached him first and killed him in his stasis.

Lust · awake · redeemed

Sorshen

Raised ~4717. Once helped engineer Emperor Xin's downfall; woke in the modern age and — astonishingly — chose redemption, allying with the heroes against Alaznist. Now rules Eurythnia within New Thassilon.

Envy · awake

Belimarius

Freed ~4718. Betrayed by Karzoug and trapped outside time in Crystilan until it shattered. Pragmatic rather than reformed, she now rules Edasseril beside Sorshen in New Thassilon — an uneasy partnership of envy and lust.

Gluttony · dormant

Zutha

Never woken. Necromancer-lord of Gastash. He has not stirred in the modern age; his relics linger, but the runelord himself still sleeps.

Sloth · dormant

Krune

Never woken. "The Quiet Lord" of Haruka. The reclusive runelord of sloth has never stirred; as of the latest word, he remains in stasis.

9 The Elevator Pitch

Sandpoint is a friendly, two-centuries-old logging-and-fishing town on Varisia's wild Lost Coast — built on a fragile peace between Taldan settlers and the Varisians who were here first, and battered by twenty years of murders, fires, goblin raids, giant attacks, sinkholes, and worse. Power has fractured from four old founding families into seven jostling factions, while just beneath the streets and out among the hills lie the ruins of Thassilon, an ancient empire of tyrant runelords whose recent reawakening has set the whole region on edge. You are local heroes the town trusts. When the elderly cleric Abstalar Zantus calls you to look into something wrong in the graveyard, you're about to discover that Sandpoint doesn't face one looming doom — it faces seven.

10 House Rules (Homebrew)

The GM's table rules for this campaign. Read these and come back with your thoughts — it's part of your homework.

New This Campaign

Sweeter Hero Points. Hero Points can now be spent on new effects (to reward initiative — like actually doing the recap):

CostYou may…
1 Hero PointReroll a damage roll · reduce an enemy's critical hit to a regular hit · negate the Death trait · sustain a spell or ability without using an action.
2 Hero PointsForce an enemy to reroll a saving throw · negate the Incapacitation trait on a player's spell or ability for one target.
3 Hero PointsSpend pre-emptively to automatically roll a critical success.
Special use · refundable

"Fuck it, We Ball"

Spend a Hero Point on a failed long-shot roll (e.g. a full-MAP attack) amid dire straits (risk of death). If it produces a tide-turning result, the Hero Point is immediately refunded.

Special use

"Use the Force, Luke"

Spend a Hero Point before a roll to roll twice and take the better result.

New rule

Doors

  • Outside combat, open doors even with hands full.
  • Open with no free hand if wielding something two-handed.
  • Open mid-Stride — spend an action at the door and continue your remaining movement; no stopping and re-Striding.
  • Exception: doors needing a Thievery or Athletics check.
New rule · earn HP

Downtime by Post

Once per week, a genuine downtime post earns a Hero Point (GM discretion). It must:

  • contain no AI-generated content;
  • be an appropriate length;
  • be genuine — memes don't count (relevant humor is fine);
  • engage the setting/NPCs or develop your character.

Collaborative posts can earn a HP for both writers.

New rule · earn HP

Recaps

Write the recap of the previous week's session and earn a Hero Point.

  • It must be posted before the day of the session — a recap posted on session day (or after) earns nothing.
  • Rewards initiative: it's the easiest Hero Point on offer.
New rule · earn HP

Vignettes

A session featuring a vignette about your character nets an extra Hero Point if you either:

  • write it yourself (after GM review, edits & approval), or
  • bring an idea and collaborate as the GM writes it.

No AI-generated content.

New rule

Map Navigation

Once initiative triggers, your token position is locked — the GM won't move it.

  • Unless you used "Stepping Away," messaged that you stepped out, or asked for a move due to Foundry trouble.
  • Down a hallway, on another floor, or standing in lava: no budging.

Trap and ambush stakes stay real.

New rule

Legacy vs. Remaster

  • Legacy content is fine if chosen intentionally.
  • Picked Legacy by accident? Swap to Remaster freely, no permission needed.
  • You can't flip-flop between the two for whichever is momentarily better.
  • Doesn't apply to class features/structure.

Standing Rules (carried over)

Standing rule

Recall Knowledge

  • Success: the name, a general description, one trait, and one useful info.
  • Critical success: all traits, plus two useful infos.
  • Retry: allowed after a failure, at +2 DC.
  • Unique creatures: succeed at the traditional creature's DC, but info is limited to that version.

"Useful info" — one of:

  • Creature level
  • A weakness/resistance/immunity
  • Its AC
  • Highest or lowest save
  • A special ability or action
  • Whether it's Weak/Elite
  • Its general strategy
  • Total HP (on a crit)
  • GM's choice
Standing rule

Combat Readiness

Have a plan before your turn starts.

  • Away from Foundry at turn start → Delay until you return.
  • Clearly undecided at turn start → Delay until you decide.
  • Read your abilities between turns; ask if unsure.
  • Use the "Step Away for a Minute" button when needed.
Standing rule · earn HP

Lightning Rounds

  • Called when a fight is clearly won and stakes are low.
  • Take turns in seconds, not minutes — delays get skipped or ended.
  • Finish without skipping anyone → every player gains a Hero Point.
  • Hero Points carry over at end of session.
Standing rule

Hero Point Cards

  • We use the optional Hero Point Deck.
  • Draw a card whenever you gain a Hero Point — you always hold cards equal to your current HP.
  • Spend a HP to use a card's ability (expends both), or for a traditional reroll (discard a card).
  • Trade at session start or in Exploration/Downtime; discard all at session's end.
GM philosophy — attacking the downed. The GM won't pick on unconscious/dying PCs "just for the heck of it," but won't artificially avoid them either (e.g. with AoE). It depends on the enemy:
  • Mindless creatures keep mauling what they just hit — but active threats pull them off.
  • Intelligent foes finish a downed PC to keep them down (especially near healing) and freely include them in AoE.
  • Animals may try to drag a body off.
  • Passionate/fanatical enemies — and undead whose abilities are fueled by kills — risk themselves for the killing blow.
Fleeing is always a valid choice.
